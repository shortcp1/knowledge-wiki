# Build vs. Buy Enterprise AI

Tracks decision frameworks and real-world outcomes for enterprises choosing between building proprietary AI capabilities vs. buying point solutions vs. using foundation model APIs directly.

Key questions tracked: What is the true total cost of a build vs. buy decision?

## Compute Infrastructure Models

### Full-Stack Labs vs. Horizontal Pooling

**Full-stack AI labs**: Vertically integrated organizations that control the entire stack from infrastructure to model development to applications. Examples include Anthropic (before broader API availability).

**Horizontal pooling**: Shared compute infrastructure model where multiple users/organizations pool resources. Enables better utilization and economies of scale but requires sophisticated orchestration.

**Trade-offs**: Full-stack integration provides tighter control and optimization but requires massive capital. Horizontal pooling enables access to frontier compute without full ownership but introduces coordination complexity.

### AMP Compute Grid Model

**Vision**: "Making FLOPs flow like megawatts" - treating compute as a fungible utility similar to electrical power.

**Independent System Operator (ISO) analogy**: Proposed evolution of compute markets toward ISO-like entities that:
- Manage dynamic prioritization across workloads
- Balance supply and demand in real-time
- Enable interruptible demand (lower-priority jobs that can be preempted)
- Coordinate across heterogeneous compute providers

**Scale targets**: AMP planning for 1.2GW base-load capacity with 6GW spike capacity - indicating utility-scale compute infrastructure.

### SF Compute and Open Protocols

Emerging initiatives around open protocols for compute marketplaces, enabling more liquid markets for AI training and inference capacity.

## Research and Development Strategy

### Research Hoarding as Market Failure

**DeepMind unpublished research**: Identified as example where research hoarding creates "negative externalities" for the broader AI ecosystem. When foundational research remains proprietary, it forces redundant discovery and slows overall progress.

**Implication for build vs. buy**: Organizations must account for research access and timing when deciding whether to build internal capabilities or rely on external providers.

## Capital Efficiency

**Too much capital too early**: Observation that excessive early-stage funding can make AI labs "fragile" - potentially by reducing operational discipline or creating misaligned incentives.

**Hardship as feature**: Anthropic's early resource constraints identified as "feature, not a bug" - forcing efficiency and focus that became cultural advantages.
