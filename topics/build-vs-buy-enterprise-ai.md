# Build vs. Buy Enterprise AI

Tracks decision frameworks and real-world outcomes for enterprises choosing between building proprietary AI capabilities vs. buying point solutions vs. using foundation model APIs directly.

Key questions tracked: What is the true total cost of a build vs. buy decision at different scales? How is the vendor landscape consolidating? When does proprietary fine-tuning justify the cost?

## Three Core Disciplines for AI Applications (Tunguz, June 2026)

Building AI applications differs from traditional SaaS. Success requires mastering three disciplines, all oriented around maximizing "intelligence per token budget":

### 1. Model Selection Strategy
- **Challenge**: Budget constraints prevent defaulting to state-of-the-art models for all use cases
- **Model Personality Differences** (as of mid-2026):
  - Kimi K2.6: Fast, excellent creative writer, less precise
  - Qwen 3.6 27b: Small model with strong performance, but unreliable ("stops suddenly in the middle of a toolchain call")
  - GLM 5.1: Excellent coding model, slower ("plodder")
- Cross-reference: [[model-architecture]] for technical characteristics

### 2. Hill-Climbing Loop Design
- **Problem definition**: Critical challenge of designing feedback loops for agentic system improvement
- **Complexity**: Requires systems design expertise (reference: Donella Meadows' systems thinking work)
- **Volatility**: Field is novel with rapidly moving models and infrastructure
- Cross-reference: [[ai-in-product-and-engineering]] for implementation patterns

### 3. Performance Evaluation
- **Challenge**: Ongoing labor to evaluate model + loop performance
- **Staffing implication**: Most companies cannot staff dedicated teams for each workflow
- **Complexity**: "AI systems are complex, finicky engines" requiring tuning of "carburetors & timing belts"
- **Vendor rationale**: Specialization allows amortization of evaluation costs across broader customer base

## Regulatory Risk Considerations

### Model Access Risk (June 2026)
- **Fable 5 Retraction**: US government shut down access to Anthropic's Fable model
- **Ecosystem Response**: Demonstrated dependency concerns:
  - Demand for model restoration
  - Calls for open-source and local model alternatives
  - Recognition of single-model dependency risk
- **Strategic Implication**: Multi-model strategy and open-weight model access become risk mitigation requirements
