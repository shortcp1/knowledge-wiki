## Global Compute Stock Trends

### Historical Growth Pattern (1970s-2023)
- **Long-term baseline**: Global compute stock grew at approximately 66% CAGR over five decades, persisting across major platform shifts (minicomputers → PCs → mobile)
- **Methodology**: Model tracking total computers of all types (mainframes, minicomputers, PCs, laptops, servers, phones, IoT devices) with bounded estimates of compute horsepower (source: Azeem Azhar, Exponential View research, June 2026)

### Historical Trend Breaks

**Mid-1990s Acceleration (1993-2006)**
- **Trigger events**: 
  - Businesses moving past Solow's paradox
  - Consumer computing expansion via Windows 95 and Internet
  - Intel Pentium (1993) with substantially improved floating-point capabilities vs. earlier x86 chips
- **Trend behavior**: Reverted to long-term mean around 2006

**Mid-2000s Deceleration Factors**
- **Dennard scaling breakdown**: Forced chip makers to multi-core architectures, which don't provide smooth FLOP capacity scaling
- **PC market maturation**: Growth plateau in personal computing
- **Mobile computing shift**: High volume but processors optimized for physical constraints rather than raw processing power
- **Cloud computing centralization**: Emphasis on efficiency over raw power; higher utilization rates over relatively lower base capacity

### AI Era Acceleration (2020-present)
- **Trend break starting 2020**: AI accelerators beginning to impact global compute stock growth
- **Distinguishing characteristic**: AI runs on floating-point operations (FLOP) as central input, driving deployment of "FLOP-factories"
- **Duration forecast**: Current accelerated pace expected to sustain "for a few years, perhaps even a decade or more" before reverting to long-term trend line (analyst estimate, moderate confidence)
- **Context**: This represents only the second major break in a 50-year trend, indicating significance of AI infrastructure buildout
