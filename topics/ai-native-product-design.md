# AI-Native Product Design

Covers how software companies are redesigning core product workflows around AI capabilities: natural language interfaces, AI-first UX patterns, ambient AI, and evolving interaction paradigms.

## Interaction Paradigm Shift

Traditionally, computing has been indistinguishable from interacting, making input methods critical to paradigm shifts. Agents fundamentally change this: they compute on your behalf without continuous interaction. A few seconds of input can trigger hours of autonomous work (theoretical). This decoupling of interaction from computation enables new device form factors and usage patterns.

## Consumer AI: State-of-Art vs. Good-Enough Threshold

**Key claim**: For consumer markets, AI doesn't need to be state-of-the-art to succeed—"good enough" functionality is sufficient. Evidence: Apple's Siri AI (2024-2025) demonstrably lags frontier capabilities (e.g., cannot autonomously execute multi-step tasks like "enter lottery when time comes") but successfully handles common consumer needs through constrained, grounded use cases.

Consumer AI requirements:
- Traditional chatbot functionality (recipes, DIY tips, image generation)
- Context awareness for personal data
- App integration (demonstrated via [[app-intents]] framework)
- Reliability over cutting-edge capability

## Grounded and Constrained Problem Spaces

Apple's approach: Focus on specific use cases where device knowledge provides unique value. Personal context from the phone grounds AI in areas less prone to errors. The problem space is:
- Constrained to high-utility scenarios
- Grounded in literal device context (what user is looking at, personal data)
- "Safe" through problem space limitation rather than capability advancement
