## Export Controls

### Mythos/Fable Export Controls (June 2026)
The U.S. Commerce Department used its authority to regulate technologies that may be national security threats to restrict exports of Anthropic's Mythos and Fable models, requiring a license for use by any foreign national, whether inside or outside of the U.S., including foreign national Anthropic employees. This led Anthropic to disable access to Fable to all users worldwide.

**Timeline and Trigger**: 
- Mythos Preview announced with restricted access (April 2026) due to advanced cybersecurity capabilities
- Fable 5 (public version with guardrails) released two months later
- Jailbreak discovered shortly after Fable 5 release, reportedly by Amazon (Anthropic investor and inference provider)
- Export control directive issued by U.S. government at 5:21pm ET (same day as jailbreak discovery)
- Directive cited national security authorities but provided no specific details of the security concern
- Government reportedly demonstrated jailbreak technique identifying "a small number of previously known, minor vulnerabilities"

**Anthropic's Position**: Company claims the identified vulnerabilities were "relatively simple" and discoverable by other publicly-available models without requiring a bypass. Anthropic argued that non-universal jailbreaks are inevitable and narrow, with no evidence of a universal jailbreak.

**Ongoing Dispute**: As of article date, senior Anthropic staff were in Washington D.C. seeking to resolve what the company characterized as a "misunderstanding," while White House officials suggested "insouciance by the company's leadership to legitimate national security concerns." Facts remain in dispute.

**Impact on AI Sovereignty**: This action significantly accelerated discussions in many nations (including U.S. allies) on AI sovereignty and how to ensure uninterrupted access to AI technology. The ability of the U.S. government to suddenly limit other nations' access to frontier AI models has increased incentives for investment in alternatives like open source models.

**Historical Parallel**: Similar to how U.S. semiconductor export controls to China accelerated China's domestic semiconductor manufacturing efforts, and China's threats regarding rare earth minerals spurred U.S. efforts to secure alternatives, the frontier model export controls are driving nations to seek alternatives to U.S.-controlled AI systems.

### The Safety-Business Paradox (Thompson Analysis, June 2026)

**Cynical Interpretation**: Critics characterize Anthropic's public statements, particularly around model releases, as "scare-mongering for the sake of marketing." The pattern of announcing models as "too dangerous" for public release, then releasing guarded versions months later, feeds this perception.

**Anthropic's Justification Framework**: The company's belief in its own commitment to safety gives it license to:
1. Aggressively favor its business interests
2. Challenge U.S. government actions
3. Release models it previously deemed too dangerous

**The Release Paradox**: If Mythos/Fable is genuinely dangerous (as Anthropic claimed), the question arises: why release Fable publicly at all, and why resist government action that aligns with stated safety concerns?

**Economic Pressures**: Analysis suggests economic imperatives drive releases despite safety concerns - labs including Anthropic and OpenAI have "collectively lost tens of billions of dollars building leading-edge models that, once released, are distilled and commoditized by open source models, primarily from China." This creates pressure to monetize capabilities before they become commoditized.

**Long-term Risk Recognition**: Observers note that even if current models (like Mythos) don't warrant drastic government intervention, "the next one will be, or the one after that, particularly now that models are increasingly useful in creating their successors" - suggesting conflict between labs and government is structurally inevitable.
