# Regulatory & Policy Landscape

Covers AI regulation and policy developments: EU AI Act implementation, US executive actions, export controls, and frontier model governance.

## Export Controls & National Security

**June 2026 Fable/Mythos shutdown:** U.S. government invoked national security authorities to issue export control directive suspending all access to Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models by any foreign national, whether inside or outside the United States. This included foreign national Anthropic employees, effectively requiring the company to "abruptly disable" these models for all customers.

**Procedural characteristics:**
- Directive received at 5:21pm ET with immediate compliance required
- Government letter did not provide specific details of national security concern
- Government cited awareness of a jailbreak method as trigger
- White House officials characterized Anthropic leadership response as "insouciance" to national security concerns

**Disputed technical basis:** Anthropic disputed the severity, stating the jailbreak only revealed "a small number of previously known, minor vulnerabilities" that were "relatively simple" and discoverable by other publicly-available models without requiring a bypass. Anthropic found no evidence of a universal jailbreak, characterizing the discovered technique as narrow.

**Foreign national restrictions:** Export controls can now be applied to prevent any foreign national access to specific models, including employees of the developing company. This represents a significant expansion of granular model-level export control enforcement.

## Model Deployment Bans

**Post-release recall authority:** U.S. government demonstrated willingness to shut down already-deployed commercial models citing national security, not just prevent initial deployment. This differs from pre-deployment testing regimes.

**Jailbreak triggers:** Discovery and demonstration of jailbreak techniques can trigger emergency government intervention, even if the jailbreak is characterized as non-universal and narrow in scope.

## Lab-Government Relations

**Escalating tensions:** Senior Anthropic staff traveled to Washington D.C. to resolve what the company characterized as a "misunderstanding" but which government officials framed as inadequate response to legitimate national security concerns (June 2026).

**Structural conflict:** Conflict between government and leading AI labs appears structurally inevitable as models become more capable and useful in creating their successors, independent of whether any particular model warrants intervention.

**Information disputes:** Major disagreements between labs and government on both procedural matters (notification, evidence sharing) and technical assessments (severity of vulnerabilities, uniqueness of capabilities).
