# AI Org Design & Headcount

Tracks how organizations are restructuring teams, roles, and reporting structures in response to AI adoption. Covers the emergence of new roles (AI engineers, prompt engineers, AI product managers), headcount reductions, and productivity-driven workforce planning.

Key questions tracked: Which roles are being reduced first? What new roles are actually being hired? How are companies thinking about AI-adjusted headcount planning?

## Software Engineering Displacement Evidence (2025-2026)

### Quantitative Employment Data

**Industry:** Software Engineering / Technology
**Business Function:** Software Development
**Source:** Arvind Narayanan & Sayash Kapoor analysis (June 2026)

**Key Finding:** No evidence of AI-driven mass layoffs in software engineering through Q1 2026.

**Specific Metrics:**
- **New York WARN Act AI Disclosure (March 2025):** First U.S. state to add AI checkbox to WARN Act filings
- **Full first year results:** 160+ companies filed WARN notices
- **AI-attributed layoffs:** 0 (not a single company checked the AI box)

**Pattern Observed:** Despite software engineering being "uniquely suited to AI disruption" with minimal regulatory barriers, employment data shows no measurable displacement effect.

### What AI Actually Automates vs. Real Bottlenecks

**Business Problem:** Understanding which aspects of software engineering are susceptible to AI automation versus which remain human-dependent.

**AI Pattern Applied:** Coding agents, code generation tools

**What AI Accelerates:**
- "Typing-code-into-a-computer phase"
- Code generation from specifications

**What Remains Human-Dependent (Real Bottlenecks):**
1. **Deciding and specifying what to build** - Product definition, requirements gathering
2. **Verifying and being accountable for what is delivered** - Quality assurance, responsibility
3. **Deep human understanding** required for both above:
   - Understanding of the codebase
   - Understanding of the business context
   - Understanding of the operating environment

**Task Breakdown Survey Findings:**
- Engineers spend significant time in meetings (not automated)
- Debugging remains a bottleneck (only partially automatable)
- The "what" and "why" questions require qualitative human judgment

### Success Factors for Human-AI Collaboration

**What Made This Pattern Succeed (Augmentation vs. Replacement):**
- AI assistance helps with deciding and verifying steps, but doesn't replace them
- Value production remains "reliant on how deeply [humans] understand both the problems and the solutions"
- Domain knowledge and context serve as durable human advantages

**Generalizability:**

The authors argue: "Given that this is true even in a sector with very few regulatory barriers, most other professions are likely to be even more cushioned."

**Logic:** If software engineering—with:
- Direct AI applicability (code is structured, formal)
- Minimal regulatory barriers
- High digital maturity
- Workers already comfortable with automation

...shows no displacement, then professions with:
- More regulatory requirements
- Less structured outputs
- More interpersonal/contextual requirements
- Higher barriers to automation

...should show even less displacement risk.

**Cross-Industry Implications:**
- Professional services requiring contextual judgment
- Roles with accountability requirements
- Positions requiring cross-functional coordination
- Jobs where "what to build" decisions are ambiguous
- Functions where domain expertise compounds over time
