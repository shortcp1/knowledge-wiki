## Empirical Evidence: AI Adoption and Headcount Growth

### Ramp/Revelio Labs Study (2026)
**Dataset**: 21,000+ US firms tracked over two years post-AI adoption (data published July 2026).

**Key findings**:
- **Heavy AI adopters grew headcount faster**: ~10% employment increase over two years for high-intensity AI firms (contradicts simple displacement narrative)
- **Entry-level roles grew fastest**: 12% growth in entry-level positions at high-AI-adoption firms
- **Skills shift hypothesis**: High-intensity AI firms appear to select for "people who know how to use AI and use it well," with entry-level workers (recent graduates, college students) a primary hiring target
- **Confidence**: High (large-N observational study with employment records)

**Proposed mechanisms for growth**:
1. **Complementarity**: AI productivity gains increase ROI per additional hire
2. **Supervision demand**: More AI-generated output requires more human review, management, and quality control
3. **Demand expansion**: Lower cost-per-task makes previously unviable tasks economically feasible, creating latent-to-actual demand conversion (parallels computing industry growth since 1970s)

**Observed organizational learning failure**: "Some firms are now learning that they fired people too quickly, mistaking task automation for human obsolescence" (source: Exponential View analysis, July 2026)

**Workflow redesign thesis**: Initial AI gains appear in individual productivity; harder gains require redesigning "entire workflows and decision-making loops." No evidence humans are unnecessary in this redesign process (as of mid-2026).

### Goldman Sachs Displacement Forecast (2026)
**Prediction**: ~9% of US workforce temporarily displaced over 10-year AI adoption transition (source: economist Joseph Briggs, cited July 2026). Note "temporarily" framing—implies eventual reabsorption or role transformation.

### Medical Sector: "Never Skilling" Risk
Medicine sector identifying "never skilling" as occupational hazard: risk that trainees relying heavily on AI never develop underlying clinical judgment. Protective measures being implemented (no specifics provided, as of July 2026).
