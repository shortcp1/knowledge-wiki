## AI Agent Framing Effects on Human Performance

### Research Finding: "AI Employees" Reduce Human Oversight Quality
A study by Emma Wiles (Boston University business professor) with 1,261 managers found significant negative impacts when AI tools are framed as "employees" or "coworkers" rather than software tools:

**Quantitative Outcomes:**
- **18% fewer errors caught** when work came from an "AI employee" vs. a chatbot
- **44% more likely to escalate** questionable work to managers rather than correcting it themselves
- **23% of companies** already list AI agents on org charts
- **Nearly 1/3 of managers** (31%) said their companies frame AI agents as employees

**What Made It Fail:**
1. **Responsibility inversion**: Participants saw themselves as less responsible for AI output when tool was framed as "employee"
2. **Over-escalation**: Managers escalated rather than correcting, negating time-saving benefits
3. **Reduced human agency**: Framing inverted sense of who's in charge
4. **Misplaced trust**: Branding created unrealistic expectations for AI capabilities

### Industry Context
**Vendor Framing (2024-2025):**
- **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang**: Promoted "digital humans" in workplaces
- **Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google**: Released AI agent management tools (April 2025+) explicitly marketed as "digital colleagues"
- Marketing emphasis on "flexibility and cognitive power of actual humans"

### Risk: AI as Blame Dumping Ground
As agents embed in critical sectors (healthcare, warfare, education, government), risk of:
- Convenient scapegoating for human decision failures
- Example cited: Iran girls' school bombing blamed on Claude, actually cascade of human errors
- Obscures bad human decisions, incentives, and oversight

### Alternative Approach: Human Augmentation Over Replacement
**Daron Acemoglu (MIT economist, 2024 Nobel Prize winner):**
> "AI agents right now are being marketed as things that can replace humans, and I think that's just a losing proposition. They should instead be optimized so that they can improve human capabilities, which is not what they have [been] at the moment."

### Worker-Centered Design Pattern
**Stanford Research Model:**
- Presented 1,500 workers across 104 jobs with AI task capabilities
- Asked what would actually be helpful (not what tech experts deemed suitable)
- **Finding**: Worker preferences often diverged from expert assumptions
  - **Law clerks**: Wanted AI for ensuring adequate case progress (accepted)
  - **Sales reps**: Did NOT want AI verifying customer credit ratings (rejected by workers, recommended by experts)

**Generalizability:**
- Pattern applies across industries: engage actual workers in AI task selection
- Bottom-up task identification vs. top-down expert assignment
- Humans retain agency and accountability for outputs

### Key Insight
Framing AI as "employees" or "coworkers" is primarily a **branding exercise** that:
1. Doesn't make tools more fit for purpose
2. Makes humans worse at their jobs
3. Erodes human agency and responsibility
4. Creates convenient blame targets for systemic failures

**Recommendation**: Frame AI as tools/assistants, not colleagues; maintain clear human accountability
