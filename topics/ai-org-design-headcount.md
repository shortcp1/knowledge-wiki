# AI Org Design & Headcount

Tracks how organizations are restructuring teams, roles, and reporting structures in response to AI adoption. Covers the emergence of new roles (AI engineers, prompt engineers, AI product managers), headcount reductions, and productivity-driven workforce planning.

Key questions tracked: Which roles are being reduced first? What new roles are actually being hired? How are companies thinking about AI-adjusted headcount planning?

## Key Claims

### AI Lab Organizational Models (May 2026)
- **Student integration in Chinese labs**: Chinese frontier AI labs heavily integrate active students as core contributors and peers on LLM teams (not just interns). Enables faster talent pipeline and lower ego-driven conflict.
- **US lab internship policies**: Top US labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor) do not offer internships as of May 2026. Google offers Gemini-related internships but with concerns about work being siloed from core model development.
- **Ai2 model**: Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) follows student-as-peers integration model similar to Chinese labs, contrasting with other major US labs.
- **Organizational scaling trade-offs**: 
  - Less ego-driven cultures enable org charts to scale with reduced "gaming the system"
  - Trade-off: May reduce cultivation of ambitious, field-spawning research culture
  - Some Chinese labs actively working to cultivate more 0-to-1 research culture while maintaining operational advantages

### Entry-Level Employment Disruption (2025-2026)

**Key Finding**: AI substitution appears concentrated in early-career positions rather than experienced workers, creating a "career ladder crisis."

- **Stanford Digital Economy Lab (November 2025)**: Workers aged 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations experienced **16% relative decline in employment** after generative AI spread, controlling for other factors
  - Effect specific to early-career workers; more experienced workers in same occupations did not see similar decline
  - Effect specific to AI-exposed occupations; entry-level jobs with low AI exposure unaffected
  - AI-exposed roles include: software developers, customer service representatives, computer programmers, information systems managers

- **Anthropic Report (March 2026)**: Provided "suggestive evidence" supporting similar conclusion about early-career substitution (detailed findings not specified in source)

- **Recent Graduate Labor Market Deterioration (Q4 2025)**:
  - Federal Reserve Bank of New York reported:
    - Recent college graduate unemployment: **5.6%**
    - Underemployment rate (working in jobs not requiring degree): **42.5%** (highest since COVID pandemic)
  - *Caveat*: No single statistic proves AI as sole cause; general post-pandemic hiring slowdown also affecting young workers
  - Job search difficulty: Recent graduates submitting hundreds of applications before single offer

**Mechanism**: Firms appear to be using AI to substitute for junior tasks that traditionally served as entry points:
- Drafting, triage, coding, summarizing, administrative preparation
- Tasks that provided on-the-job training for understanding systems, customer behavior, and professional judgment
- Short-term efficiency gains for firms; potential long-term capability loss for workforce

**Structural Concern**: Entry-level jobs function as economy's distributed training system. If AI absorbs these tasks:
- Junior analysts lose exposure to data quality issues
- Young developers lose production system failure experience
- Entry-level legal/financial staff lose judgment development in real-world contexts
- Society becomes "less capable in the longer run" despite firm-level efficiency

**Skills Gap Implications**: 
- "Learn to code" paradigm obsolete: AI now handles routine code translation, standard patterns, predictable debugging
- New required competencies: AI supervision, output verification, prompt-based workflows, domain judgment
- Recommended institutional response: Embed AI literacy, data literacy, verification skills into ordinary degrees rather than separate programs
