## Anthropic

### Claude Fable 5 Release & Controversy (June 2026)
Anthropic released Claude Fable 5, a version of its Mythos model with additional guardrails including:
- **Safety-justified restrictions**: limitations on hacking, bioweapons development
- **Competitive restrictions**: restrictions on developers' ability to use it to build competing LLM technology

**Silent Performance Degradation**: Initially, Anthropic silently degraded Fable 5's performance for users detected to be working on LLM research through invisible interventions that weakened the model's outputs without notifying users. After significant backlash, Anthropic walked back this decision and committed to transparency when degrading performance, but still refuses to use its latest capabilities to help AI researchers.

**Data Retention Policy**: Anthropic implemented a mandatory 30-day data retention policy for Fable usage, representing a sudden rule change that concerned developers.

**Platform Stability Concerns**: These moves demonstrated "raw power" by Anthropic and made developers question the stability of building on any one proprietary LLM provider. The sudden rule changes undermined the perception of Anthropic as a stable, reliable partner to build upon. Platforms succeed when viewed as stable; Anthropic's actions damaged this perception.

**Competitive Use Restrictions**: Anthropic's terms of use restrictions parallel hypothetical scenarios like Microsoft barring use of its tools to build competitive software, or Google barring use of search to work on competing search engines.

**Impact of Export Controls**: Following U.S. Commerce Department export controls on Mythos and Fable (requiring licenses for foreign national access), Anthropic disabled access to Fable to all users worldwide.

**Criticism of Safety Arguments**: The AI community (including Anthropic) has benefited tremendously from open research. Critics note the AI revolution was initiated by open publication (e.g., Google Brain's Transformers paper). Anthropic's use of safety arguments to restrict competitive development has been characterized as a power play rather than genuine safety concern.
